\textbf{Privacy Attack.}

\textbf{Membership inference attack.} Membership inference attack was first introduced by using a Shokri et al.~\cite{shokri2017membership} where it necessasites a shadow model that imitate the behavior of the target model and an attack model to distinguish member/non-member based on the target model's behavior.  Whereas current attack methods all require access to the model's predicted confidence score, Choquette~\cite{choquette2021label} introduce a label-only attack that instead evaluates the robustness of the model's predicted (hard) labels under per- turbations of the input, to infer membership. Salem et al.~\cite{salem2018ml} relax the assumption of using shadow model (not knowning knowledge of the target model structure) and  having a dataset from the same distribution as the target model's training data and showing that such attacks are very broadly applicable at low cost and thereby pose a more severe risk than previously thought. Yeom reveals \cite{yeom2018privacy} that overfitting is sufficient to allow an attacker to perform membership inference. Song et al. \cite{song2019privacy} indicate defensive methods against adversarial examples increases the target model's vulnerability against membership inference attacks. Ye et al. \cite{ye2022enhanced} uses shadow model, which improves by using self-distillation, which further reduce the uncertainty of the attacker about the training data.
Liu et al.\cite{liu2022membership} exploit target sample’s loss trajectory from the whole training process of the target model leveraging knowledge distillation for improving the attack performance
