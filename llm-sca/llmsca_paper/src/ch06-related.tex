Determining the provenance and protecting the intellectual property of deep learning models has traditionally relied on watermarking techniques~\cite{protectItelligentPropertyDeepNeuralNetworksWatermarking}. Early methods introduced watermarks by embedding backdoors into the network during the training phase~\cite{TurningYourWeaknessIntoStrength}. While effective for verifying ownership, contemporary watermarking approaches for Large Language Models (LLMs) primarily rely on embedding specific statistical patterns into the generated text to identify ownership from outputs~\cite{kirchenbauer2024watermarklargelanguagemodels,yang2023watermarkingtextgeneratedblackbox,christ2023undetectablewatermarkslanguagemodels,kuditipudi2024robustdistortionfreewatermarkslanguage}. However, this inherently alters the output distribution, frequently sacrificing the model's generation quality and practical utility.

\noindent\textbf{Model Attribution and Similarity Metrics.} 
To avoid the continuous generation degradation inherent to watermarks, active model fingerprinting proactively injects query-response triggers via instruction tuning~\cite{xu-etal-2024-instructional, russinovich2025heythatsmodelintroducing}, adversarial prompt suffixes~\cite{trap}, stealthy Chain-of-Thought extractors~\cite{CoTSRF}, or output statistics~\cite{RoFL}. However, these intrusive modifications remain susceptible to detection, performance degradation, and fine-tuning-induced forgetting, which exacerbates as model scales increase~\cite{nasery2025scalablefingerprintinglargelanguage}. Consequently, research has shifted toward non-intrusive, post-hoc similarity metrics. These approaches initially focused on dynamic runtime inference, evaluating pairwise similarity through black-box output distributions~\cite{pasquinillmmapfingerprintinglargelanguage} or white-box activation and gradient statistics~\cite{zhang2024reefrepresentationencodingfingerprints, wu2025gradientbasedmodelfingerprintingllm}. Because dynamic inference incurs prohibitive forward-pass computational overhead, recent methods transition to static weight analysis. While early static techniques trace full training trajectories for smaller architectures~\cite{neuralLineage}, LLM-centric approaches extract cross-layer statistical consistencies~\cite{yoon2025intrinsicfingerprintllmscontinue} or utilize Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)~\cite{wang2025ghosttransformerdetectingmodel}. To further resist adversarial permutation and scaling attacks, state-of-the-art static methods compute robust structural invariants~\cite{huref} and orthogonal embedding relations~\cite{matrix, wu2026llmdna}. Despite these advancements in extraction efficiency and robustness, all aforementioned metrics are fundamentally limited to strictly pairwise similarity evaluation.

\noindent\textbf{Ecosystem-Scale Model Lineage Reconstruction.} 
Beyond pairwise verification, pioneering works scale similarity metrics to reconstruct macroscopic evolutionary graphs of the open-source ecosystem, aiming to uncover complex $N$-to-$N$ genealogical trees~\cite{rahman2025hugginggraphunderstandingsupplychain}. Initial black-box methods leverage dynamic inference to compute vocabulary probability differences, such as Nei's genetic distance, for phylogenetic tree construction~\cite{yax2025phylolminferringphylogeny}. However, relying purely on distance yields unrooted trees that fail to infer evolutionary directionality and often misidentify root models. To resolve this lack of directionality, recent white-box approaches incorporate intrinsic or extrinsic signals. Specifically, they utilize weight-based $L_2$ distances combined with layer kurtosis~\cite{horwitz2025unsupervised} or metadata timestamps~\cite{horwitz2025charting} to construct directed lineages, accurately charting how foundation models adapt and branch over time.

\noindent\textbf{Layer-wise Dynamics in Transformers.}
Understanding the layer-wise internal mechanisms of Transformers has long been a focal point for model interpretability and architecture optimization. While early studies analyzed the hierarchical progression of linguistic features across hidden layers~\cite{van_Aken_2019,kornblith2019similarityneuralnetworkrepresentations}, recent evidence suggests that Transformer components exhibit significant functional specialization and asymmetric sensitivity during model evolution. For instance, Feed-Forward Networks (FFN) are increasingly recognized as key-value memories that store factual knowledge~\cite{geva-etal-2021-transformer}, while attention sub-layers (Q, K, V) govern contextual alignment. The success of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) further underscores this heterogeneity, demonstrating that updates to specific matrices like $W_q$ and $W_v$ are often sufficient to capture the majority of model shifts~\cite{lora}. In the context of model provenance, specific weight blocks have been found to maintain invariant spectral signatures during reuse~\cite{wang2025ghosttransformerdetectingmodel}, yet existing lineage detection frameworks largely treat all parameters as a homogeneous vector space when calculating inter-model distances. It remains unexplored how individual components--namely the Q, K, V, and MLP matrices--differentially contribute to the fidelity and robustness of macroscopic lineage graph reconstruction.
