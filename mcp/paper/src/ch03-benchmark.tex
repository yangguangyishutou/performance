\subsection{Construction}

The construction of our benchmark involves a systematic pipeline that collects and validates open-source large language models on Hugging Face. This process is divided into two core components: automated model crawling and filtering, and strict verification of direct fine-tuning parent-child relationships. Every step follows explicit, evidence-based rules without arbitrary assumptions or decisions.

\paragraph{Component 1: Model Crawling and Filtering}
We develop a dedicated Python crawler to automatically collect model metadata from Hugging Face. Using the official API, we extract comprehensive information including model name, author, download count, model card content, base model, architecture, and derivation descriptions. All collected raw data is structured and saved in standardized JSON files for unified management and subsequent processing.

To ensure the credibility and reliability of experimental models, we perform quantitative filtering on the crawled dataset. The core filtering criterion is the **download count** of each model, which serves as an objective metric to evaluate practical adoption and trustworthiness. Only models exceeding the pre-defined download threshold are retained for downstream experiments, eliminating low-quality, rarely-used, or untested models. This component ensures our benchmark consists of representative and practically meaningful models.

\paragraph{Component 2: Direct Fine-Tuning Relationship Verification}
A critical goal of our benchmark is to identify valid parent-child pairs where the child model is **directly fine-tuned from the parent model**. To avoid erroneous relationships from automated model trees, we design a strict three-step verification pipeline:

First, we examine the official model card of each child model. If the model card explicitly states that the model is "directly fine-tuned from a specific base model", we mark the parent-child relationship as valid.

Second, for models without clear annotations in the model card, we directly contact the original developers via email to confirm the authentic derivation source.

Finally, we classify all candidate pairs into three categories based on verification results:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Confirmed Valid}: Explicitly labeled or author-confirmed direct fine-tuning pairs → retained.
    \item \textbf{Confirmed Invalid}: Explicitly denied by authors → excluded.
    \item \textbf{Unconfirmed}: No response from developers → marked as uncertain and excluded.
\end{itemize}

Notably, we \textbf{never rely solely on model tree relationships} for judgment, as they may provide incomplete or misleading structural links instead of genuine fine-tuning processes. This manual+authoritative validation ensures the ground-truth quality of parent-child relationships in our benchmark.

\subsection{Characteristics}

Our benchmark exhibits several notable characteristics that reflect its diversity, reliability, and authenticity for LLM genealogy analysis:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Credible Model Sources}: Models are selected based on objective download counts, ensuring all experimental samples are widely used, representative, and trustworthy in real-world applications.
    
    \item \textbf{Genuine Fine-Tuning Relationships}: Unlike automated crawling pipelines, our benchmark uses model card annotations and developer confirmation as dual validation, eliminating false parent-child pairs from model trees.
    
    \item{Strict Non-Arbitrary Rules}: Every filtering and validation step follows pre-defined, evidence-based criteria. No relationships are inferred or assumed without explicit support, ensuring full reproducibility.
    
    \item \textbf{High Data Quality}: Through multi-step verification and error exclusion, the benchmark provides high-integrity parent-child model pairs for model derivation analysis.
    
    \item \textbf{Real-World Relevance}: The benchmark reflects authentic community-driven fine-tuning practices, making it suitable for research on model inheritance, composition, and genealogy.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metrics}


\textbf{Precision and Recall.} Precision evaluates the proportion of true vulnerabilities among those identified by the model. Recall reflects the proportion of correctly detected vulnerabilities among all ground-truth vulnerabilities. F1-score measures the harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced assessment of the model's detection performance.


\textbf{Matched Pair Accuracy.} Traditional precision and recall metrics cannot fully capture whether the model genuinely distinguishes between positive and negative samples based on underlying code semantics, or merely relies on superficial matching of specific API calls. Leveraging our paired benchmark, where each vulnerable sample is matched with its fixed counterpart, we employ Matched Pair Accuracy (MPA)~\cite{matchedpair}. A pair is deemed correct only when both instances are classified correctly. MPA thus captures the proportion of pairs for which the model simultaneously identifies the vulnerable and non-vulnerable samples correctly.
